I am a Postdoctoral Research Fellow at Massachusetts General Hospital and the Broad Institute of MIT and Harvard, working with Drs. Mark Daly and Benjamin Neale. My research focuses on developing statistical methods to understand how the genetic bases of complex diseases varies across ancestry, sex, and time, with the goal of improving personalized risk prediction.

I am currently developing frameworks to model genetic effects on the X chromosome and leading analyses within the Global Biobank Meta-Analysis Initiative to uncover the genetic basis of disease progression. I also collaborate closely with the International Lung Cancer Consortium on translational studies in lung cancer genetics.

I received my PhD in Biostatistics from Harvard University, advised by Dr. Xihong Lin, where I developed scalable methods for polygenic risk prediction. I received my BSE in Operations Research and Financial Engineering from Princeton University, with a certificate in Statistics and Machine Learning, where I was advised by Dr. Jianqing Fan.
